If you or someone with you is in danger — struggling to breathe, unresponsive,
having chest pain or a seizure, or at risk of harming themselves or someone
else — contact emergency services now.

You do not need to be certain it is serious to call. That is what they are for.

If the situation is frightening but not life-threatening, a warm line or crisis
line can talk it through with you in real time. The numbers below are kept
current.

## The next morning

Danger does not always announce itself during the experience. If it is the
day after and any of this is true, that is a reason to call, not to wait:

- Distress that has not lifted, or is getting worse instead of better.
- Sleep that has not come back after a full day.
- Perceptual changes — visuals, a feeling of unreality — that have not faded.
- Any thought of hurting yourself or someone else.

If you are reading this the day after and still do not feel safe, that is a
reason to call, not to wait.
