If you sat with someone through an intense experience, the day after has
its own shape for you: long hours, someone else's fear held steady against,
things you heard that you are now carrying. That residue is not a footnote
to their experience, and it does not need to be smaller than theirs.

Two ways of responding tend to land badly, and naming them makes them
easier to catch yourself in: waving it off — "you're fine, it was just a
trip" — and inflating it, treating the person as permanently changed, or as
someone whose every sentence now carries a message. Both close down the
conversation they might actually want to have.

What people describe as helpful is plainer: being heard without judgment —
not interpreted, and not advised. In one survey of 608 people with extended
difficulties after psychedelic use, talking with peers or community was the
support most often reported as helpful (Robinson et al., 2024). You do not
need an interpretation to have been useful.

Your side counts the same way. If what you sat with is still sitting with
you, the [resources](/resources) page — peer lines, circles, directories —
is available to you as well, and [Support & safety](/safety) is always
there.
