In a clinical or ceremonial setting, someone else usually handles the room:
the music, the temperature, whether there's food when it's needed. Without
that person, those small things become yours to set up in advance — and
they matter more than they sound like they should.

Music specifically has a documented role. Research on psychedelic-assisted
therapy has described music as functioning almost like a second, hidden
guide during a session, shaping the emotional tone of the experience
directly. A playlist downloaded ahead of time, so it plays without a
signal or an ad breaking in, is a small thing that does real work.

The Western-classical, ambient-drone sound that shows up in a lot of
clinical playlists is a convention some researchers built, not a finding
about what works best for you. If a different kind of music genuinely
settles you, that's a reasonable choice too.

Beyond music, a few physical basics tend to matter:

- Water within reach, and food that needs no cooking or decisions later.
- A blanket or something warm — temperature regulation can shift.
- A space that's already comfortable, so you're not making it comfortable
  while also managing everything else.

None of this is a ritual you have to get right. It's just easier to arrange
before, when arranging things is easy, than during, when it isn't.
