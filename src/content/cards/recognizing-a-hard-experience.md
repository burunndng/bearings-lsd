Difficult experiences are common enough to have been studied directly, and
a range of specific phenomena show up often enough in that research to be
worth naming plainly. If any of this matches something you went through,
it has been documented before. You're not the only one, and it isn't a
sign that something unusual or uniquely wrong happened to you.

Some of what's been described in that research:

- Fear that felt disproportionate to any real danger present.
- A feeling of losing control, or of not being able to stop what was
  happening.
- Grief, or an encounter with loss, sometimes unrelated to anything
  specific in your life.
- A feeling of isolation — as though no one else could reach you or
  understand what was happening.
- Physical distress: nausea, tension, or discomfort with no clear cause.
- Paranoia, or a sense that something or someone meant you harm.
- Insight or realization that felt overwhelming rather than clarifying.
- A feeling that something about your sense of self had changed, or that
  you couldn't locate who you normally are.

This list isn't a checklist and there's no score attached to it. Naming
something as a known, studied, common phenomenon is the point — not
measuring how much of it happened to you. If you want a private place to
write about any of this for yourself, [Notes](/notes) is there, and it
stays on this device.

If distress from something like this hasn't lifted after a few days, that
is worth taking seriously. See [Support & safety](/safety).
