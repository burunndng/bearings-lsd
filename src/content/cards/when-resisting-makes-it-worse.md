A common piece of advice for a hard moment is some version of "don't
resist, let it move through you." There's a real basis for this: work
drawing on psychological flexibility models has found that fighting an
unpleasant experience often intensifies it, while allowing it to be
present, without needing to fix or escape it, can loosen its grip.

That framing genuinely helps some people, some of the time. It is not a
rule, and it is not the only valid response to a hard moment.

For some people — particularly with certain trauma histories — being told
to "surrender" or "let go" during an already frightening or disorienting
experience can feel like being told their instinct for self-protection is
wrong. It isn't. If a moment feels unsafe, you are allowed to act on that:

- Sit up. Turn the lights on. Open a window.
- Say out loud, to yourself or someone else, that you want it to stop or
  slow down.
- Get up and move, if moving helps you feel more in your body, not less.

"Working with it" and "getting yourself to a place where you feel safer"
are not opposites. You can do the second thing first, and decide about the
first thing after.
