Plenty of people do this alone, on purpose or because that is what their
life allows. It is a real and common choice, not a lesser one. It is also
worth being plain about what it removes.

Being alone removes a second set of eyes if something goes wrong
physically. It removes someone who can call for help if you cannot. It
removes a person who can simply tell you, later, what happened, if your own
memory of it is unclear. Clinical research on hallucinogens has
consistently used trained supervision as a core part of managing risk —
not because participants were fragile, but because a present, sober person
is one of the more reliable ways to catch a problem early.

None of that means solo use is unsafe by definition. It means the things a
present person would normally catch become entirely yours to plan for
instead.

You may want to consider:

- Telling one person, even remotely, roughly when you're starting and when
  they should expect to hear from you again.
- Keeping your phone charged and reachable, in case you need it or someone
  needs to reach you.
- Choosing a space where you won't need to unlock a door, drive, or deal
  with anyone else showing up.
- Writing down, ahead of time, what you'd want to happen if things went
  badly — see the Sheet for a place to do exactly that.

If anything about your situation makes solo use feel like more risk than
you want to hold, pausing costs nothing. See [Consider pausing or
reconsidering](/safety) for what else is worth weighing.
