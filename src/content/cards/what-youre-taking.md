Substances sold as one thing are sometimes something else, or something
else mixed in. This is not a reason to panic — it is a reason to check, if
checking is available to you.

Reagent test kits (Marquis, Mecke, and similar) can help rule out some
substitutions, and fentanyl test strips can detect fentanyl contamination
specifically. Neither tells you dose, purity, or that something is safe.
A clean reagent result is information, not a guarantee.

Local drug-checking services, where they exist, can do more — some use lab
equipment to identify what is actually present. Harm-reduction organizations
in your area are usually the fastest way to find out what is available near
you and how to use it correctly.

This page will not tell you an amount to take. Amounts vary by substance,
by person, and by source, and a wrong number here could cause harm rather
than prevent it. Testing what you have is a separate, safer question than
how much of it to take, and testing services are the right place for both.

None of this removes risk. It reduces the risk of a specific, preventable
kind of harm: not knowing what you actually have.
