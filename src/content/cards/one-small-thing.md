It's common, in the days after a psychedelic experience, to arrive at a big
resolution: to completely change how you live, work, relate to people, or
see yourself. Some of that clarity is real. Most sweeping resolutions,
though, tend to fade within days or weeks, once ordinary life resumes and
the felt intensity of the experience recedes.

Work on how people actually integrate meaningful experiences points toward
something more modest and more durable: not a total transformation, but
one specific, concrete change, small enough to actually do. Calling one
person. Changing one habit. Saying one true thing you'd been avoiding.

Nobody owes the experience a transformed life. If something small and real
came out of it, that's not a lesser outcome than the big story you might
have expected — it's often the part that survives.
