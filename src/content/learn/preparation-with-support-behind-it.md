"Set and setting" — mindset going in, and the physical and social
environment around the experience — is one of the older ideas in this
field, and it holds up reasonably well against more recent research.
Studies looking at what predicts how a psychedelic experience goes have
found that factors present before anything starts, like expectation and
readiness, correlate with people's later reports of how manageable a
psychedelic experience felt. That is consistent with a placebo-adjacent
mechanism: context and expectation shape experience, which is a real
effect, not a dismissal of the experience itself.

Separately, a psychological flexibility framework — being willing to have
a difficult feeling rather than needing to control or avoid it — has been
proposed as one lens for supporting people through psychedelic-assisted
work. It is one model among several, not a settled consensus, but it maps
onto something practical: acceptance-based coping tends to serve people
better in the moment than the alternative of fighting what is happening.

None of this is about mindset controlling outcome. Research on
implementation intentions — plans made ahead of time in a specific
if-this-then-that structure — has found they help people carry through on
decisions made in a calmer state precisely because deliberation is harder
in the moment itself. That is a mechanism for why preparation done in
advance, not improvised in the moment, is the more reliable kind. See
[Deciding things early](/card/deciding-things-early) and the Sheet for
what that looks like in practice.
