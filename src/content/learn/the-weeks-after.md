"Integration" gets used loosely, but as a concept in the literature it
describes something specific: the ongoing process of making sense of an
experience and folding whatever is useful from it into daily life, over
time, rather than in one sitting. Analyses of how integration is actually
practiced describe it as a process, not an event, and one that keeps going
well past the days immediately after.

That matters because a common pattern is a rush of insight or resolve in
the first days after an experience, followed by that resolve fading
without daily life ever visibly changing. This isn't evidence that the
experience "didn't work" or that the person did integration wrong. Sudden
sweeping resolutions are just hard to sustain by nature, whether they come
from a psychedelic experience or anywhere else. Insight and behavior
change are related but separate things, and the second one is slower and
less dramatic than the first.

What tends to hold up better than a grand resolution is something small
and concrete: one specific, ordinary change, repeated, rather than a
declared transformation. See [One small thing](/card/one-small-thing) for
what that looks like. There is no deadline on any of this, and no
requirement that anything change at all — see [I had a good
time](/card/that-is-enough) for the other valid outcome.
