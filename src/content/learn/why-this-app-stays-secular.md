Some psychedelic substances and practices come out of specific Indigenous
and ceremonial traditions, with their own histories, protocols, and
knowledge holders. This app is not a spiritual leader, does not claim
authority over any of that, and does not present ceremonial content
without the community that holds it being directly involved, credited, and
compensated. Where that hasn't happened, we default to saying nothing
rather than borrowing the aesthetics without the substance.

That is a different thing from being value-neutral about everything else.
Discussion of psychedelic-assisted therapy and harm reduction raises real
ethical and legal questions — informed consent, scope of practice, who
gets to offer support and under what training — and those questions
deserve direct, adult treatment rather than a workaround. That work exists
on the clinical and harm-reduction side of the field, and it is possible
to engage with it seriously without adopting a specific cosmology or
telling anyone what their experience is supposed to mean.

So when this app avoids phrases like "sacred container" or "trust the medicine," voice:allow
that isn't squeamishness about spirituality. Some people using this app do
hold spiritual or ceremonial frameworks for their own experience, and that
is entirely theirs to hold. What this app won't do is hand out someone
else's frame as if it were the default, or dress up a wellness-brand
aesthetic in borrowed language that doesn't come from anywhere real.
Secular and specific beats vague and borrowed.
