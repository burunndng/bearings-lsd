Some experiences benefit from being looked at more than once, from more than
one angle. What follows are four lenses. They come from different traditions
— analytic psychology, integral theory, transpersonal psychology, and Hermetic
symbolism — and they do not agree with each other about what is real. Each is a
lens, not a verdict. You can use one, several, or none, in any order.

## At a glance

| Lens | The plain question |
|---|---|
| **Image** — Jung | What picture shows up when you remember it, without deciding what it means? |
| **Shape** — Integral (AQAL) | Where in your life is this landing — inside, in your body, with others, or in practical things? |
| **State** — Transpersonal | Is this vivid for now, or is it becoming part of everyday life? |
| **Symbol** — 777 | Is there a symbol that stands in for this? You don't need one. |
| **Monday question** | What wants to be carried forward into an ordinary week, and what can be left as it is? |

Each lens has a short section below — what it asks, and where it came from.

## Image — what keeps returning

Some people find a single image keeps returning — a doorway, a figure, an
animal, a scene. If it feels right, write it down without explaining it, and
let it answer a couple of questions on paper: who are you, what do you want,
what do you fear? The image is a way of holding what happened so
it can be looked at. It is not a claim about what happened.

If an image starts to feel like it *is* the truth of the experience, you can
set it aside for a while. Or you can keep it — that is
allowed too. Ambiguity is a complete outcome. The companion card,
*"A doorway that keeps returning,"* on the integration shelf offers one place to
capture a single image, if you want it.

## Shape — where it lands

Any experience touches more than one part of a life. You might ask which
parts this one is touching:

- **Inner sense** — what it felt like from inside
- **Body and behavior** — sleep, appetite, movement, warmth and rest in the
  hours after
- **People around you** — who feels close, who understands, who does not
- **Practical matters** — time, money, access, work, the place you are in

An experience that touched only one part is not a failure. You might notice
it asking to be carried somewhere it has not reached yet.

## State — passing or settling

Some of what comes back feels like a passing state — vivid for a while, then
part of the past. Some feels like it is settling into ordinary days, changing
how everyday things feel. Both are complete ways for something to land.

Not every intense moment needs to become a durable change. A moment that was
simply had is not wasted, and no one should suggest otherwise. If a feeling
of "I am supposed to become something now" shows up, it is worth noticing
that too — nobody owes the experience a better self.

Research on integration practices is still early, and there is no controlled
evidence that any single approach reliably produces change. The practices
described here are low-risk and commonly practiced; they are not treatments with
a demonstrated effect.

## Symbol — an optional mnemonic

*The correspondences in this section draw on Hermetic Qabalah, a Western
esoteric synthesis codified by Crowley in 777. It is offered as symbolic
language, not as a religious claim or a factual structure.*

Some people who already work symbolically find that assigning an experience a
letter, an element, a planet, or a color gives them a place to stand with it.
As one example: the Qabalistic Tree of Life maps ten stations from crown
through wisdom, understanding, beauty, foundation, and kingdom — the ordinary
world. A person who holds an insight as "crown" can ask what it would look
like by the time it reached "kingdom": what changes as it travels down?

You may want to consider this only if you already work this way. If no symbol
comes, that is not a gap. If many symbols come, that is not proof.

## The plain question

If any of this matters to you, the question it tends to come back to is:
what, if anything, does this point toward in an ordinary week — and what can
be left alone? Answering is optional. Not answering is also complete.
